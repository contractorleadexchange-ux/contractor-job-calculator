Washington contractor permit guide

When contractors can use a Washington reseller permit—and when they cannot

A permit lets qualifying contractors make certain purchases for resale without paying retail sales tax to the seller. It is not a blanket exemption for everything bought for a job. Use the examples and documentation checklist below before ordering materials or paying subcontractors.

1. What a contractor reseller permit is for

Washington DOR issues reseller permits to qualifying businesses, including contractors performing retail or wholesale construction. A valid permit generally supports tax-free wholesale purchases of eligible materials and subcontract labor purchased for resale. The contractor later accounts for its own taxable construction sale to the customer as required.

For typical custom construction, qualifying project materials are those that become part of the completed real-property improvement: for example, permanently installed roofing, lumber, drywall, wiring, pipe, concrete and insulation. Qualifying contract labor can include services purchased from a subcontractor for resale as part of the custom-construction project.

Think “incorporated into the job,” not simply “used on the job.” DOR distinguishes permanently incorporated construction materials from tools, equipment and supplies the contractor consumes while performing work.

3. Worked example: a roofing materials order

Suppose a contractor doing an otherwise qualifying taxable residential roofing job orders $6,000 of shingles that will be installed permanently, plus $500 of equipment rentals and consumable supplies. The contractor has a current valid reseller permit and gives a copy to the applicable supplier for the qualifying shingles.

For ordinary custom construction, the $6,000 installed material purchase may qualify as a wholesale purchase with the permit. The $500 equipment and consumable purchase normally does not: retail sales tax or use tax rules still apply as appropriate. Do not apply the permit to the entire order merely because everything is charged to a customer job.

Separately, when the contractor bills the homeowner for the qualifying custom-construction service, it generally calculates retail sales tax on the full taxable contract price—not solely on the materials portion. See the contractor sales-tax invoice example.

4. How to apply, verify and maintain the permit

  1. Apply through My DOR: construction businesses must apply for the contractor reseller permit; DOR requests information about material and contract labor purchases. DOR says the permit application is free.
  2. Use the current document: a Washington contractor reseller permit is generally valid for two years, compared with four years for many other businesses. Verify actual dates shown on the permit.
  3. When you are the buyer: give a valid permit to the seller only for qualifying purchases for resale, and keep purchase records linking eligible items to the relevant job.
  4. When you are the seller/subcontractor: obtain and verify the customer's valid permit or an approved exemption document before claiming wholesale treatment. Keep evidence supporting each wholesale sale.
  5. Retain documentation: DOR instructs contractors accepting reseller permits to retain the permits for five years. Follow DOR's current documentation rules for each sale and verification record.

Also review the general permit rules and verification options. If a buyer cannot provide acceptable proof, a wholesale seller generally must collect retail sales tax unless another exemption applies.

5. Mistakes that can become expensive

  • Using the permit for tools or rental equipment: purchases the contractor consumes usually are not qualifying resale purchases even if billed to the project.
  • Assuming a prime's word is enough: wholesale treatment requires appropriate valid documentation; keep the actual evidence.
  • Using permits on special construction categories: government construction, public-road work and speculative building may not qualify under ordinary retail/wholesale construction rules.
  • Ignoring paid sales tax on otherwise qualifying purchases: DOR describes possible tax-paid-at-source deductions or refunds in qualifying situations. Check eligibility and records rather than simply deducting sales tax from another bill.

Washington DOR warns that improper use can result in additional tax, a 50% permit-misuse penalty, and possible revocation. Do not infer that merely having a permit makes a purchase tax-free.

Frequently asked questions

Does a contractor have to pay for the reseller permit?

No. Washington DOR says an eligible contractor can apply without an application charge.

Can a contractor buy power tools or rent equipment using the permit?

Generally no when the tools or rentals are used by the business in performing work rather than resold as qualifying inventory.

How long is a contractor reseller permit valid?

Generally two years, but rely on the dates and status of the actual permit.

Can a subcontractor skip collecting sales tax because its buyer says it is a prime?

Not based only on that assertion. Obtain a valid reseller permit or other approved exemption document and verify the transaction qualifies.

Does this apply to public road projects or speculative building?

Not as a blanket rule. Washington DOR identifies special classifications for those projects that require a different analysis.
