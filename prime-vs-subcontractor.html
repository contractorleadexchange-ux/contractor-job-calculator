Prime contractor vs. subcontractor: who collects Washington sales tax?

For typical custom construction, the buyer and your documentation—not simply the work you perform—help determine whether a sale is retail or wholesale. Walk through a home-remodeling example before you invoice your next project.

1. Start with the relationship, not the trade

Prime contractor: When a homeowner directly hires a contractor to improve the homeowner's real property, the job is generally treated as retail custom construction. The prime normally collects sales tax on the total taxable contract price and reports the job under the retailing B&O classification.

Subcontractor: A contractor performing qualifying custom construction for a prime generally sells its services at wholesale when the prime provides a valid reseller permit or other acceptable exemption documentation. The sub then ordinarily does not charge retail sales tax on that qualifying wholesale invoice and generally reports wholesaling B&O.

No valid resale documentation? DOR generally directs a sub to collect retail sales tax unless a different exemption is properly established. Failing to document wholesale sales can trigger additional tax liability. See the contractor reseller permit checklist.

2. Worked example: one remodel, two invoices

Illustrative example, not a current local tax quote: A property owner agrees to pay a prime contractor $24,000 before sales tax for a taxable custom-remodeling job. The prime hires an electrical subcontractor for $8,000 as part of the job and gives the sub a valid reseller permit. For illustration only, assume the combined sales-tax rate at the job site is 8.9%. Always verify the actual rate for the specific job address.

How the two construction transactions are usually treated

Prime → property owner$24,000$2,136 (8.9%)Retailing
Electrical sub → prime, valid permit on file$8,000$0 on qualifying wholesale saleWholesaling

The homeowner's hypothetical bill is $26,136, including sales tax. The prime does not automatically subtract its $8,000 subcontract expense from its taxable retail selling price. The sub's qualifying wholesale sale is a separate transaction supported by its documentation.

At the simplified state B&O rates used in this site's job calculator, the $24,000 retailing receipt produces an estimated $113.04 (0.471%); the $8,000 wholesaling receipt produces an estimated $38.72 (0.484%). Those are two different businesses' estimates, before any available credits, deductions or local taxes. They are not the amount of sales tax collected from the homeowner.

What if the sub does not obtain a valid reseller permit or another approved document? The transaction may need to be treated as retail, so the sub should determine the tax to collect before sending its invoice. Do not assume that because the prime will eventually charge the homeowner sales tax, the sub can skip its documentation.

The word “general contractor” on a business license does not by itself determine the tax on every invoice. Consider the specific transaction and project.

4. Before the estimate becomes an invoice

  1. Record the property owner, actual construction address, who owns the land, and who hired your business.
  2. Check the project against DOR's Construction Tax Matrix.
  3. If selling wholesale, obtain and verify the buyer's current reseller permit or another acceptable document and retain the evidence.
  4. If selling retail, look up the correct job-site sales-tax rate; state sales tax separately where required.
  5. Estimate B&O using the appropriate classification without treating collected retail sales tax as profit.

Frequently asked questions

Does a subcontractor always charge the prime sales tax?

No. Qualifying subcontract work sold with valid reseller-permit or other accepted exemption documentation generally is a wholesale sale without retail sales tax collected from the prime.

Does a prime avoid sales tax on labor by listing it separately?

Generally no for taxable custom construction: the total retail construction selling price, including labor, is normally taxable.

Should the prime charge sales tax only on the amount after paying the sub?

Generally no. For ordinary taxable custom construction, the prime's retail selling price to the owner is the relevant sales-tax base, not the price minus subcontract expenses.

Is a reseller permit appropriate for every kind of construction?

No. Speculative building, public road construction, U.S. government contracting and other special activities require separate review under DOR guidance.
