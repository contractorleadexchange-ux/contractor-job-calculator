Washington contractor tax guide

Washington contractor sales tax: what to charge and where

Before submitting a construction bid, identify the buyer, the type of work, and the job location. Those facts help determine whether Washington retail sales tax applies and which combined state and local rate to use.

1. Start with the buyer and the type of construction

For an ordinary custom construction job, a prime contractor hired by the property owner to improve property it does not own generally makes a retail sale. The contractor generally collects retail sales tax on the full pre-tax contract price unless a specific exemption applies. Itemizing labor, materials, permits, overhead, or profit normally does not remove them from that taxable amount.

A qualifying subcontractor doing work for a prime may be making a wholesale sale. To support not collecting retail sales tax, the subcontractor generally needs a valid reseller permit from the buyer or other appropriate documentation. Without it, different treatment may apply.

Different Washington rules can apply to speculative construction, public roads, federal government contracting, logging roads and other special categories. Classify the project before issuing the bid.

Ask before pricing: Who hired you: the property owner or another contractor? Who owns the land? Is this a special type of construction? If you are a subcontractor, do you have proper resale documentation?

2. Use the job-site rate, not your office rate

Retail construction services are generally sourced to where the work is performed. A contractor based in one city may need a different combined state and local rate for a project in another city, even within the same county. Look up the rate for the specific job address.

Check Washington DOR sales-tax rates

For example, 0.089 is the decimal form of 8.9%. This is an illustrative rate, not a claim about any particular location's current tax rate.

3. Worked example: an $18,500 custom-construction invoice

Assume a homeowner hires a prime contractor for a taxable remodeling job. The agreed pre-tax contract price is $18,500. For this hypothetical invoice only, assume the combined job-site sales-tax rate is 8.9%.

Hypothetical taxable construction invoice
Line itemAmount
Pre-tax contract price for materials, labor and agreed work$18,500.00
Retail sales tax: $18,500 × 0.089$1,646.50
Total billed to homeowner$20,146.50

The $1,646.50 is tax collected for remittance; it is not added job profit. Estimate profit from the pre-tax $18,500 contract price minus appropriate job costs, overhead and business taxes.

What if the contract says “tax included”?

Washington DOR says sales tax generally must be separately stated on qualifying custom-construction contract documents or billings. Simply writing “tax included” is not enough to establish that the quoted price includes sales tax. If you intend a tax-inclusive total, work out the pre-tax amount and separately state the actual tax before signing.

4. Reseller permits do not exempt every purchase

For qualifying custom construction, a contractor may generally buy materials that become a permanent part of the finished project and eligible subcontractor services for resale using a valid reseller permit. Tools, certain rentals, equipment and supplies consumed in doing the work are normally not wholesale purchases for resale, even when charged through to the customer.

Keep the appropriate permit records when making or accepting qualifying wholesale sales. Different project types may require a different sales/use-tax approach.

Four checks before sending the bid

  1. Classify the work: standard custom construction or a special category?
  2. Confirm the buyer: landowner or prime; obtain proper wholesale records if applicable.
  3. Verify the job address: look up the correct combined rate.
  4. Separate tax on the invoice: do not confuse collected tax with job revenue.

Frequently asked questions

Is labor taxable on a Washington construction job?

For ordinary taxable custom construction sold by a prime to a property owner, retail sales tax generally applies to the total selling price, including the labor portion.

Can I use the sales-tax rate at my office?

Not merely because your office is there. Retail construction services are generally sourced to where the job is performed.

Does a subcontractor always charge sales tax to a general contractor?

No. Qualifying wholesale subcontract sales supported by a valid reseller permit generally do not require the subcontractor to collect retail sales tax. Proper records and project classification matter.

Can this calculator determine all exemptions or file my tax return?

No. It is a simplified job-planning tool, not a legal classification engine or tax-filing service.

Important: public road construction, federal government contracting, speculative building, exemptions and other project types can follow different rules. Confirm the classification before relying on this general treatment.
