Washington contractor B&O tax: price for gross-receipts tax

A construction job may generate Washington Business & Occupation tax even when it earns little or no profit. See how the common retailing and wholesaling classifications change your job-pricing estimates.

1. Why gross receipts matter to a contractor's bid

Washington state Business & Occupation (B&O) tax is generally measured on gross receipts under the applicable tax classification—not on a contractor's profit—subject to authorized deductions and credits. Direct job costs typically cannot simply be subtracted before applying the B&O rate.

Take a hypothetical $18,500 pre-sales-tax custom-construction contract. If direct job costs are $15,000, the job produces $3,500 in gross profit before overhead and B&O. At the 0.471% retailing rate, estimated state B&O is $87.14 ($18,500 × 0.00471), before credits or deductions. Gross profit after that rounded estimated tax is $3,412.86, before overhead and other expenses.

Even if unexpected costs eliminate all $3,500 of that gross profit, the simplified gross-receipts tax estimate remains $87.14 if the same taxable receipts and classification apply. That is why B&O belongs in job-pricing planning.

2. Retailing vs. wholesaling: same receipts, different classification

The state rates used in the site's simplified calculator, checked against Washington DOR in October 2026, are 0.471% retailing and 0.484% wholesaling. These categories describe different types of sales; contractors cannot choose whichever produces the smaller tax estimate.

3. What changes the tax classification?

  • Who hired you? A property owner hiring a prime for ordinary custom construction typically indicates retailing. A prime buying qualifying subcontract work for resale typically indicates wholesaling.
  • Who owns the land? Building on your own land as a speculative builder may follow a different tax structure.
  • What is the project? Federal government jobs, public roads, and other special construction types can use different rules.
  • What documentation do you hold? Valid reseller-permit records generally support qualifying wholesale sales.

Review the Prime vs. Subcontractor Guide, Reseller Permit Guide and the Sales Tax Guide before modeling unfamiliar job categories.

4. What the simplified calculator does not handle

It does not model all Washington state deductions, tax credits, local/municipal B&O taxes, special construction classifications, federal income tax, employment taxes, loan payments, or actual net business profit. The Washington small-business B&O tax credit may lower the amount eligible businesses ultimately owe, depending on their circumstances.

Use the simplified calculation for early pricing and reserves; use your records and current Department of Revenue instructions to decide your actual tax classification, deductions, credits and filing liability. Frequently asked questions

Can I deduct field labor and materials before calculating B&O?

Ordinary cost-of-doing-business expenses are generally not subtracted from gross receipts to determine B&O. Specific statutory deductions and credits can still apply.

Is separately collected retail sales tax part of my job revenue?

Retail sales tax collected for remittance is not earned contract revenue for the simplified gross-margin examples on this site.

Can I pick the wholesaling rate instead of retailing?

No. The facts of the sale and the applicable documentation determine classification.

Is the displayed B&O estimate my final tax bill?

No. It is a rough job-pricing estimate. State credits, deductions, local taxes and special project rules may change the actual amount.

Calculator limitation: it does not model every deduction, credit, municipal tax, payroll tax, workers' compensation cost, federal tax or project-specific classification. Use it for job-planning estimates, not as a tax return.
